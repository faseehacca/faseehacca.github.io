### Study Hub Activity 1

**Suggest FOUR reasons why an entity may wish to change its auditor.**

1. **Better Value for Money and Wider Services:** The entity may change its auditor to obtain better value for money, lower audit fees, or access to a broader range of professional services from one firm (one-stop shopping).
2. **Poor Service or Working Relationship:** The entity may be dissatisfied with the current auditor due to poor service, lack of responsiveness, delays, or an ineffective working relationship.
3. **Need for Specialist Expertise:** The entity may require an audit firm with greater industry knowledge, international experience, or specialist expertise because of business growth or complex transactions.
4. **Change in Ownership or Management:** A takeover, merger, management buyout (MBO), or change in senior management may result in the appointment of a new auditor preferred by the new owners or management.
5. **Audit Firm Merger or Conflict of Interest:** If the audit firm merges with another firm, a conflict of interest may arise, or the client may believe the new firm is too large to provide a personal service.
6. **Audit Firm Ceases to Operate:** The entity must appoint a new auditor if the existing audit firm closes down or ceases trading.
7. **Reputation or Stakeholder Expectations:** Large companies, especially listed entities, may appoint a multinational audit firm because investors, lenders, or regulators expect auditors with a strong international reputation.
8. **Auditor Rotation Policy:** The entity may have a policy of changing auditors after a specified period (e.g., every 10 years) to maintain auditor independence.
9. **Existing Auditor does not seek Reappointment:** The current auditor may decide not to stand for reappointment at the end of the audit engagement.
10. **Death or Incapacity of the Auditor:** If the auditor is a sole practitioner and dies or becomes incapable of performing the audit, the entity must appoint a replacement auditor.

### CR 1

You are an audit supervisor of Cupid & Co, planning the final audit of a new client, Prancer Construction Co, for the year ending 30 September 20X7. The company specializes in property construction and providing ongoing annual maintenance services for properties previously constructed. Forecast profit before tax is $13.8m and total assets are expected to be $22.3m, both of which are higher than for the year ended 30 September 20X6.

You are required to produce the audit strategy document. The audit manager has met with Prancer Construction Co’s finance director and has provided you with the following notes, a copy of the August management accounts and the prior year financial statements.

**Meeting notes**  
The prior year financial statements recognize work in progress of $1.8m, which was comprised of property construction in progress as well as ongoing maintenance services for finished properties. The August 20X7 management accounts recognize $2.1m inventory of completed properties compared to a balance of $1.4m in September 20X6. A full year-end inventory count will be undertaken on 30 September at all of the 11 building sites where construction is in progress. There is not sufficient audit team resource to attend all inventory counts.

In line with industry practice, Prancer Construction Co offers its customers a five-year building warranty, which covers any construction defects. Customers are not required to pay any additional fees to obtain the warranty. The finance director anticipates this provision will be lower than last year as the company has improved its building practices and therefore the quality of the finished properties.

Customers who wish to purchase a property are required to place an order and pay a 5% non-refundable deposit prior to the completion of the building. When the building is complete, customers pay a further 92.5%, with the final 2.5% due to be paid six months later. The finance director has informed you that although an allowance for receivables has  
historically been maintained, it is anticipated that this can be significantly reduced.

**Information from management accounts**  
Prancer Construction Co’s prior year financial statements and August 20X7 management accounts contain a material overdraft balance. The finance director has confirmed that there are minimum profit and net assets covenants attached to the overdraft.

A review of the management accounts shows the payables period was 56 days for August 20X7, compared to 87 days for September 20X6. The finance director anticipates that the September 20X7 payables days will be even lower than those in August 20X7.

**Describe the process Cupid & Co should have undertaken to assess whether the PRECONDITIONS for an audit were present when accepting the audit of Prancer Construction Co - (3 marks)**

**Answer:**
The preconditions for an audit are based on the requirements of International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board **ISA 210 – Agreeing the Terms of Audit Engagements**.
##### Process to assess whether the preconditions for an audit were present

1. **Determine whether an acceptable financial reporting framework will be used**
    - Cupid & Co should confirm that Prancer Construction Co prepares its financial statements using an acceptable financial reporting framework (e.g., International Financial Reporting Standards).
2. **Obtain management's agreement to its responsibilities**
    - Management should acknowledge and accept responsibility for:
        - Preparing the financial statements.
        - Maintaining adequate internal controls.
        - Providing the auditor with access to all relevant information, records, and unrestricted access to employees.
3. **Assess whether there are any limitations on the audit**
    - Cupid & Co should ensure there are no restrictions imposed by management that would prevent obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence. If such limitations exist and cannot be resolved, the firm should not accept the engagement.

### CR 2

Salt & Pepper & Co (Salt & Pepper) is a firm of Chartered Certified Accountants which has seen its revenue decline steadily over the past few years. The firm is looking to increase its revenue and client base and so has developed a new advertising strategy where it has guaranteed that its audits will minimize disruption to companies as they will not last longer than two weeks.

In addition, Salt & Pepper has offered all new audit clients a free accounts preparation service for the first year of the engagement, as it is believed that time spent on the audit will be reduced if the firm has produced the financial statements.

The firm is seeking to reduce audit costs and has therefore decided not to update the engagement letters of existing clients, on the basis that these letters do not tend to change much on a yearly basis.

One of Salt & Pepper’s existing clients has proposed that this year’s audit fee should be based on a percentage of their final pre-tax profit. The partners are excited about this option as they believe it will increase the overall audit fee.   
Salt & Pepper has recently obtained a new audit client, Cinnamon Brothers Co (Cinnamon), whose year end is 31 December. Cinnamon requires their audit to be completed by the end of February; however, this is a very busy time for Salt & Pepper and so it is intended to use more junior staff as they are available.

Additionally, in order to save time and cost, Salt & Pepper have not contacted Cinnamon’s previous auditors.

**Required:**

**Describe the steps that Salt & Pepper should take in relation to Cinnamon:**
**Prior to accepting the audit - (5 marks)**

**Answer:**
##### 1. Professional Clearance

Salt & Pepper should:

- Ask Cinnamon's management for permission to contact the existing auditor.
- Contact the outgoing auditor, asking for all information relevant to the decision whether or not to accept appointment (e.g. overdue fees, disagreements with management, breaches of laws and regulations).
- If a reply is not received, try to contact the outgoing auditor by another means such as telephone.
- If a reply is still not received, Salt & Pepper may still choose to accept the engagement but should proceed with care.
- If a reply is received, consider the outgoing auditor's response and assess whether there are any ethical or professional reasons why the appointment should not be accepted.
- If Cinnamon refuses permission for the outgoing auditor to respond, Salt & Pepper should normally decline the engagement.
##### 2. Independence and Objectivity

Salt & Pepper should consider whether they are completely independent of Cinnamon Brothers Co. If any threats to independence or objectivity cannot be reduced to an acceptable level, the audit engagement should not be accepted.
##### 3. Management Integrity

Salt & Pepper should consider the integrity and reputation of Cinnamon's directors. A lack of integrity may indicate risks such as:

- Aggressive interpretation of accounting standards, including window dressing, management bias and inappropriate judgements.
- Weak control environment and possible management override of controls.
- Intimidation of auditors.
- Criminal activities such as fraud, money laundering or breaches of laws and regulations.
- Aggressive tax evasion or avoidance.
- Unreliable management representations.
##### 4. Money Laundering

Salt & Pepper must comply with money laundering regulations. If there is suspicion of, or evidence of, money laundering by Cinnamon Brothers Co, the audit engagement should not be accepted.
##### 5. Resources

Salt & Pepper should consider whether there are adequate staff, time and skills available to perform the audit properly.

In this scenario, Cinnamon requires the audit to be completed by the end of February, which is a busy period for Salt & Pepper. The firm should therefore assess whether sufficient experienced staff are available, as relying mainly on junior staff could reduce audit quality.
##### 6. Risks

Salt & Pepper should consider the level of risk attached to auditing Cinnamon Brothers Co. Any risks identified should be evaluated, as they increase the engagement risk and the risk of issuing an inappropriate audit opinion.
##### 7. Fees

Salt & Pepper should consider whether the expected audit fee is acceptable and adequate for the work and risk involved. They should also consider Cinnamon's creditworthiness, as non-payment of fees could create a self-interest threat.
##### 8. Professional Competence

Salt & Pepper should only accept the audit if they have the necessary knowledge, skills and experience to perform the engagement competently and meet Cinnamon's reporting deadline.
##### 9. Reputation of the Client

Salt & Pepper should consider the reputation of Cinnamon Brothers Co and whether association with the company could damage the firm's own reputation.
##### 10. Preconditions for an Audit (ISA 210)

Before accepting the engagement, Salt & Pepper must establish that the preconditions for an audit are present and that there is a common understanding with Cinnamon's management.

They should confirm that:

- An acceptable financial reporting framework will be used.
- Management accepts responsibility for:
    - Preparing the financial statements.
    - Maintaining internal controls.
    - Providing the auditor with all relevant information and unrestricted access to records and personnel.

### CR 3

Salt & Pepper & Co (Salt & Pepper) is a firm of Chartered Certified Accountants which has seen its revenue decline steadily over the past few years. The firm is looking to increase its revenue and client base and so has developed a new advertising strategy where it has guaranteed that its audits will minimise disruption to companies as they will not last longer than two weeks.

In addition, Salt & Pepper has offered all new audit clients a free accounts preparation service for the first year of the engagement, as it is believed that time spent on the audit will be reduced if the firm has produced the financial statements.

The firm is seeking to reduce audit costs and has therefore decided not to update the engagement letters of existing clients, on the basis that these letters do not tend to change much on a yearly basis.

One of Salt & Pepper’s existing clients has proposed that this year’s audit fee should be based on a percentage of their final pre-tax profit. The partners are excited about this option as they believe it will increase the overall audit fee.   
Salt & Pepper has recently obtained a new audit client, Cinnamon Brothers Co (Cinnamon), whose year end is 31 December. Cinnamon requires their audit to be completed by the end of February; however, this is a very busy time for Salt & Pepper and so it is intended to use more junior staff as they are available.

Additionally, in order to save time and cost, Salt & Pepper have not contacted Cinnamon’s previous auditors.

**Required:**
**Describe the steps that Salt & Pepper should take in relation to Cinnamon:**
**To confirm whether the preconditions for the audit are in place - (3 marks)**

**Answer:**
##### 1. Confirm an Acceptable Financial Reporting Framework

Salt & Pepper must determine whether the financial reporting framework to be applied in the preparation of Cinnamon Brothers Co's financial statements is acceptable.
In considering this, the auditor should assess:

- The nature of the entity.
- The nature and purpose of the financial statements.
- Whether law or regulation prescribes the applicable financial reporting framework.
##### 2. Obtain Management's Agreement to Its Responsibilities

Salt & Pepper must obtain Cinnamon's management's acknowledgement and understanding of its responsibility for:

- **Preparing the financial statements** in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, including, where relevant, their fair presentation.
- **Establishing and maintaining internal controls** necessary to enable the preparation of financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- **Providing the auditor with:**
    - Access to all relevant information for the preparation of the financial statements.
    - Any additional information requested by the auditor.
    - Unrestricted access to persons within Cinnamon from whom the auditor considers it necessary to obtain audit evidence.
##### 3. If the Preconditions Are Not Present

If the preconditions for an audit are not present, Salt & Pepper should discuss the matter with Cinnamon's management.
Unless required by law or regulation, Salt & Pepper must not accept the audit engagement if:

- The financial reporting framework to be used is unacceptable.
- Management does not acknowledge and accept its responsibilities for the preparation of the financial statements, internal controls, and providing the auditor with the necessary information and access.

### CR 4

In agreeing the terms of an audit engagement, the auditor is required to agree the basis on which the audit is to be carried out. This involves establishing whether the preconditions for an audit are present and confirming that there is a common understanding between the auditor and management of the terms of the engagement.  

**Required:**
**Describe the process the auditor should undertake to assess whether the PRECONDITIONS for an audit are present - (3 marks)**

**Answer:**
##### 1. Determine Whether the Financial Reporting Framework Is Acceptable

To assess whether the preconditions for an audit are present, the auditor must determine whether the financial reporting framework to be applied in the preparation of the financial statements is acceptable.
In considering this, the auditor should assess:

- The nature of the entity.
- The nature and purpose of the financial statements.
- Whether law or regulation prescribes the applicable financial reporting framework.
##### 2. Obtain Management's Agreement to Its Responsibilities

The auditor must obtain management's acknowledgement and understanding of its responsibility for:

- **Preparing the financial statements** in accordance with the applicable financial reporting framework, including, where relevant, their fair presentation.
- **Establishing and maintaining internal controls** necessary to enable the preparation of financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- **Providing the auditor with:**
    - Access to all relevant information for the preparation of the financial statements.
    - Any additional information requested by the auditor.
    - Unrestricted access to persons within the entity from whom the auditor considers it necessary to obtain audit evidence.
##### 3. If the Preconditions Are Not Present

If the preconditions for an audit are not present, the auditor should discuss the matter with management.
Unless required by law or regulation, the auditor must not accept the proposed audit engagement if:

- The financial reporting framework is unacceptable.
- Management's agreement to its responsibilities has not been obtained.

### CR 5

**Describe the steps an audit ﬁrm should perform prior to accepting a new audit engagement. (5 marks)**

**Answer:**

The firm will consider the following matters before accepting a new engagement or client:

- Professional clearance
- Independence and objectivity
- Management integrity
- Money laundering
- Resources
- Risks
- Fees
- Professional competence
- Reputation of the client
- Preconditions of an audit
- Continuance
##### Professional Clearance

If offered an audit role, the prospective audit firm must:

- Ask the client for permission to contact the existing auditor
- Contact the outgoing auditor, asking for all the information relevant to the decision whether or not to accept appointment (e.g. overdue fees, disagreements with management, breaches of law and regulations)
- If a reply is not received, the prospective audit firm should try and contact the outgoing auditor by other means such as telephone
- If a reply is still not received, the prospective auditor may still choose to accept but proceed with care
- If a reply is received, consider the outgoing firm's response and assess if there are any ethical or professional reasons why they should not accept the appointment
- The existing auditor must ask the client for permission to respond to the prospective auditor
- If the client refuses permission, the existing auditor should notify the prospective auditor of this fact
##### Independence and Objectivity

Auditors need to consider whether they are completely independent of the prospective client. If the assurance provider is aware, prior to accepting an engagement, that the threats to objectivity cannot be managed to an acceptable level, the engagement should not be accepted.
##### Management Integrity

A lack of integrity may indicate risks such as:

- Aggressive interpretation of accounting standards including window dressing of financial statements, management bias and inappropriate judgements.
- Weak control environment and possible override of controls.
- Intimidation of auditors
- Criminal activities such as money laundering, fraud, breach of laws and regulations
- Aggressive tax evasion/avoidance
- Unreliable management representations
##### Money Laundering

The firm must comply with money laundering regulations. If there is a suspicion of money laundering, or actual money laundering committed by the prospective client, the firm cannot accept the engagement.
##### Resources

The firm must consider whether there are adequate resources (staff, time and skills) available at the time the engagement is likely to take place to perform the work properly. If there is insufficient time or resources, the quality of work could be affected.  
Money is not a resources for audit firm, it can be reimbursed as audit fee.
##### Risks

Any risks identified with the prospective client such as poor performance, poor controls, unusual transactions, should be considered. These risks can increase the level of engagement risk i.e. the risk of issuing an inappropriate report.
##### Fees

The firm should consider the acceptability of the fee. The creditworthiness of the prospective client should also be considered as non-payment of fees can create a self-interest threat.
##### Professional Competence

An engagement should only be accepted if the audit firm has the necessary skill and experience to perform the work competently.
##### Reputation of the Client

The audit firm should consider the reputation of the client and whether its own reputation could be damaged by association.
##### Preconditions of an Audit

ISA 210 _Agreeing the Terms of Audit Engagements_ and the _Code of Ethics and Conduct_ provides guidance to the professional accountant when accepting new work.

Before accepting an engagement, the auditor must establish whether the preconditions for an audit are present and that there is a common understanding between the auditor and management.

Is the financial reporting framework acceptable?

- Consider the entity and the purpose of the financial statements
- State which financial reporting framework should be used

Do management accept their responsibilities?

- For preparing financial statements
- For internal controls
- For giving the auditor all relevant information they request
##### Continuance

Once the engagement is complete, the audit firm must revisit the acceptance considerations again to ensure it is appropriate to continue for the following year.

### CR 6

**List FOUR examples of matters the auditor may consider when obtaining an understanding of the entity.**

**Answer:**

1. **Market and Competition**
    - The market in which the entity operates and its competitors.
2. **Legislation and Regulations**
    - The laws and regulations affecting the entity.
3. **Regulatory Framework**
    - The regulatory environment governing the entity's operations.
4. **Ownership of the Entity**
    - The ownership structure and those charged with governance.
5. **Nature of Products, Services and Markets**
    - The entity's principal products/services and the markets it serves.
6. **Location of Production Facilities and Factories**
    - The geographical location of manufacturing plants, offices, or operating sites.
7. **Key Customers and Suppliers**
    - The entity's major customers, suppliers, and business relationships.
8. **Capital Investment Activities**
    - Significant investments in property, plant, equipment, or other long-term assets.
9. **Accounting Policies and Industry-Specific Guidance**
    - The accounting policies adopted and any industry-specific accounting requirements.
10. **Financing Structure**
	- Sources of finance, borrowings, overdrafts, and capital structure.
11. **Significant Changes Since Prior Years**
	- Changes in ownership, management, operations, systems, or business activities compared with previous years.

### Study Hub Activity 2

Your firm has been invited by Mr.Thorburn, managing director and majority shareholder of Carling Co, to accept appointment as auditor of the company. The present audit firm will not be reappointed when its term of office expires, as Mr.Thorburn is dissatisfied with its services.

In addition, Mr.Thorburn has requested that:

1. an employee of your firm assumes responsibility for preparing the monthly management accounts to a tight deadline. The continuation of the overdraft facility is dependent on receipt of these accounts within 10 days of each month end; and
2. the audit partner attends the monthly board meetings, mainly to explain the management accounts to the other directors.

**(a) Describe the matters that you should consider in deciding whether to accept appointment as auditor and provide the additional services requested.**

**Answer:**
**Reason for dissatisfaction:** The reason behind the change in appointment could indicate that it may not be appropriate to accept the nomination. For example, if the present auditor firm has:

- modified the current year’s audit opinion; or
- declined to provide the additional services (e.g. on ethical grounds).

**Permission to communicate:** If permission to communicate freely with the current auditor is not granted, the nomination should be declined.

**Inherent risk:** Factors which are likely to increase inherent risk include:

- Reliance by the bank on the audited financial statements (as well as the unaudited management accounts); and
- The dominance of Mr.Thorburn as the owner-manager.

**Objectivity:** Objectivity in relation to the audit assignment could be threatened by:

- Mr.Thorburn’s dominance (as majority shareholder he controls the audit appointment);
- The preparation of monthly management accounts (depending on the degree to which they are integrated with financial information);
- The audit partner appearing to assume an executive role at board meetings.

**Human resources:** Depending on the time taken to produce the management accounts (it could be the first week of every month), there may be no suitable employee available after taking into account study leave, holidays and commitments to existing clients.

The audit partner’s attendance at monthly board meetings could also be time consuming depending on the amount of preparation required.

**Expertise:** Depending on the nature of Carling’s business and the complexity of its cost and management accounts, there may not be an employee or audit partner with the necessary expertise available to provide the additional services.

**Responsibilities:** Responsibility for the preparation of management accounts must be assumed by the management. The audit partner could not assume responsibility for any explanations given to the directors.

**Package of services:** Mr.Thorburn expects a range of services to be provided by the company’s auditor. It is possible that, in declining to provide the additional services, Mr.Thorburn would not make the audit appointment.

**Fees:** The fees for the additional services could easily exceed the audit fee. It may not be possible for Mr.Thorburn to justify such costs, especially when the company is dependent on the bank’s support. Also, the company may not be able to pay promptly for the audit and additional services provided.

**(b) List the actions you would take before reaching a decision whether you should undertake any assignments for Carling.**

**Answer:**

- Discuss with Mr.Thorburn the reason(s) for his dissatisfaction.
- Obtain Mr.Thorburn’s permission to communicate freely with the current auditor. If refused, decline the nomination.
- Write to the current auditor to enquire of any professional reason why the nomination should not be accepted.
- Consider the incumbent’s response. For example, if it casts doubt on Mr.Thorburn’s integrity (e.g. because he has not explained the real reason for change), the nomination should be declined.
- Explain to Mr.Thorburn the respective responsibilities of directors and auditors in relation to financial and management accounts and that these would be included in a letter of engagement.
- Briefly review the management accounts to ascertain:
    - the level of expertise and time required to prepare them;
    - to what extent they are integrated with financial information.
- Review the bank overdraft facility and correspondence with the bank to assess the degree of going concern risk.
- Review employee’s suitability for the preparation of management accounts and their availability.

**(c) Assuming that there are no professional reasons why the audit appointment should not be accepted, reach a conclusion on whether you should provide the additional services requested.**

**Answer:**

Assuming that there are no professional reasons why the appointment should not be accepted:

1. Management accounts may be prepared (subject to staff availability) provided that:
    - Management assumes full responsibility for them
    - the employee is not involved in the audit assignment
2. It is unlikely that the audit partner could attend the board meetings as this would be seen to detract from objectivity.

### Study Hub Activity 3

During your preliminary planning of an audit client, you note that the engagement letter has been returned, unsigned, by the directors. When asked to explain their action, the directors indicate that they cannot allow you access to information on the company’s new website development as this contains commercially sensitive information. You will not, therefore, be able to perform audit procedures on the research and development expenditure incurred on the website that is presented in non-current assets in the statement of financial position.

**Explain the actions you should take as a result of the directors’ refusal to sign the engagement letter.**

**Hold Further Discussions with the Directors**
Initiate further discussions with the directors to understand their concerns more fully and attempt to reach a resolution that allows access to sufficient appropriate audit evidence without compromising genuinely commercially sensitive information.

**Remind the Directors of Their Responsibilities**
Remind the directors of their statutory responsibilities under company law and ISA 210 – Agreeing the Terms of Audit Engagements, which require management to provide the auditor with access to all information and explanations necessary to perform the audit.

**Explain That This Is a Limitation of Scope**
Explain that refusing access to information relating to the website development constitutes a limitation of scope, preventing the auditor from obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence.

**Explain the Effect on the Audit Opinion**
Explain that if the research and development expenditure recognized as non-current assets is material, and sufficient appropriate audit evidence cannot be obtained to confirm the accounting treatment, the audit opinion will need to be modified.

**Consider Whether the Preconditions for an Audit Are Met**
If the directors continue to refuse to sign the engagement letter and restrict access to audit evidence, assess whether the preconditions for an audit have been met. If the preconditions are not present, the auditor should decline the engagement (unless required by law or regulation to accept it).

### Study Hub Activity 4

**Suggest FIVE factors that may make it appropriate for the engagement letter to be revised.**

**Answer:**

1. **Misunderstanding of the audit scope or objective**: If it becomes clear that the client has misunderstood the objective or scope of the audit, the engagement letter should be revised to remove any ambiguity.
2. **Revised terms of the engagement**: Any changes in the agreed audit terms (e.g., additional responsibilities or changes in reporting requirements) require an updated engagement letter.
3. **Changes in senior management**: For example, the appointment of new directors may require the engagement letter to be reissued to ensure continued agreement with audit terms.
4. **Significant change in the nature or size of the business**: Expansion, restructuring, or diversification of the entity’s operations may affect the audit engagement and require revision of the letter.
5. **Change in legal or regulatory requirements**: Updates in law or regulation (e.g., references in the engagement letter becoming outdated such as Companies Act 1985 being replaced by Companies Act 2006) require revision.
6. **Change in the financial reporting framework**: For example, switching from local GAAP to IFRS or another reporting framework.
7. **Change in other reporting requirements**: Any additional or altered reporting obligations imposed on the auditor or entity.
